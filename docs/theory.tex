\title{Coordinating Distributed Energy Resources Behind One Meter\\
\large Theory notes for \texttt{home-energy-optimizer}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A home with solar panels, batteries, a water heater, a heat pump and an
electric vehicle has several distributed energy resources (DERs) behind one
meter. Planning them together, so that the electricity bill plus the
occupants' discomfort is as small as possible, couples them only through the
meter, where energy bought costs more than energy sold. These notes state that
problem and the method \texttt{home-energy-optimizer} uses to solve it. Each DER is
planned by its own solver --- a linear, quadratic or dynamic programme, or any
other method --- provided it answers three queries of a fixed form. A
\emph{coordinator} then makes the DERs' plans agree at the meter. The default
coordinator, Dantzig--Wolfe, blends the DERs' plans in a small linear programme
and bounds how far the result can be from the best plan; the alternative, ADMM,
needs no linear programme and steers the DERs with one shared price, but gives
no such bound. The result is a way to make DERs from different makers
interoperate within a house: together they reach a better outcome than each
would alone, and the saving is shared so that each is fairly rewarded for its
part. We also describe how a plan is executed and the prices it comes with. Every number quoted
is reproducible with a script in \texttt{bench/}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Time and data}

The horizon is split into $n$ slots of $\dt$ hours, $t = 0,\dots,n-1$
(typically $\dt = 0.25$~h over $24$--$48$~h). A slot at power $p$ moves
$p\,\dt$ kWh. The forecasts are the import and export prices $\cimp_t$ and
$\cexp_t$, the household load $\ell_t$ and the PV output $g_t$. Their
difference $d_t = \ell_t - g_t$ is the \emph{inflexible net demand}: what the
meter would see with no controllable device. A device may need forecasts of its
own --- hot-water draw for a water heater, outdoor temperature for a heat
pump --- which only it uses.

\subsection{Devices}
\label{sec:devices}

Device $k$ ($k = 1,\dots,m$) draws power $p_{k,t}$ from the meter in slot $t$,
negative when it supplies; its \emph{plan} is $p_k = (p_{k,0},\dots,p_{k,n-1})
\in \R^n$. Everything else about it is private, and summarised by two objects:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item $\Pk \subset \R^n$, the plans it can run, given its physics and its state
  now;
\item $f_k : \Pk \to \R$, its \emph{private cost}: everything except the
  electricity bill --- discomfort, the value of energy left in store at the end
  of the horizon, a missed charging goal.
\end{itemize}
Each device modelled here has one state variable, which evolves with its power
from a measured initial value. The constraints below hold for every slot $t$;
Appendix~\ref{app:models} derives them.

\paragraph{Battery} (state: stored energy $s_t$; reserve $\underline S$ and
capacity $\bar S$; power limits $\bar P_{\mathrm c}, \bar P_{\mathrm d}$;
one-way efficiencies $\Ec, \Ed$; terminal price $\pi_{\mathrm T}$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  &\text{dynamics:}    && s_{t+1} = s_t + \dt\,\psi(p_t), \qquad
                          \psi(p) = \Ec\,p \ \text{ if } p > 0, \quad \psi(p) = p/\Ed \ \text{ if } p \le 0, \\
  &\text{constraints:} && -\bar P_{\mathrm d} \le p_t \le \bar P_{\mathrm c}, \qquad \underline S \le s_t \le \bar S, \\
  &\text{private cost:}&& f(p) = \pi_{\mathrm T}\,(s_0 - s_n).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:battery}
\end{equation}
Charging stores only $\Ec$ of the energy drawn; each kWh delivered drains
$1/\Ed$ from the store. Energy drawn down over the horizon is charged at
$\pi_{\mathrm T}$ (the lowest import price by default), so energy left in store
is worth $\pi_{\mathrm T}$ per kWh.

\paragraph{Water heater} (state: water temperature $T_t$; element power $P$;
thermal capacitance $C$; setpoint $T^\star$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  &\text{dynamics:}    && T_{t+1} = T_t + \tfrac{\dt}{C}\big(P\alpha_t - Q^{\mathrm{out}}_t(T_t)\big), \\
  &\text{constraints:} && a_t \in \{0,1\} \ \text{(or $[0,1]$ if modulating)}, \quad
                          \alpha_t = \min\{a_t, \bar\alpha_t(T_t)\}, \quad p_t = P\alpha_t, \\
  &\text{private cost:}&& f(p) = \dt\textstyle\sum_t \pi^{\mathrm{wh}}_{\mathrm K}\,[T^\star - T_{t+1}]^+ \;+\; C\bar c\,[T^\star - T_n]^+ .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:tank}
\end{equation}
$a_t$ is the command (off or on), $\alpha_t$ the fraction of the slot the
element actually runs, $\bar\alpha_t$ the thermostat's cut-out, and
$Q^{\mathrm{out}}_t$ the heat lost through the insulation and carried off by the
hot water drawn (Appendix~\ref{app:tank}). The last
term charges a tank left short at the end the cost of reheating it.

\paragraph{Heat pump / HVAC} (state: room temperature $T_t$; electrical power
$P$; coefficient of performance $\mathrm{COP}$; room capacitance
$C_{\mathrm{room}}$ and envelope resistance $R_{\mathrm{wall}}$; comfort band
$[T^{\mathrm{lo}}_t, T^{\mathrm{hi}}_t]$, which may change over the day):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  &\text{dynamics:}    && T_{t+1} = T_t + \tfrac{\dt}{C_{\mathrm{room}}}\Big(q(a_t)\,\alpha_t
                          + \tfrac{T^{\mathrm{out}}_t - T_t}{R_{\mathrm{wall}}}\Big), \\
  &\text{constraints:} && a_t \in \{-1, 0, +1\} \ \text{(cool, off, heat)}, \qquad
                          \alpha_t = \min\{|a_t|, \bar\alpha_t(T_t)\}, \qquad p_t = P\alpha_t, \\
  &\text{private cost:}&& f(p) = \dt\textstyle\sum_t \pi^{\mathrm{hv}}_{\mathrm K}\,\xi_{t+1}(T_{t+1})
                          \;+\; (1\,\mathrm h)\,\pi^{\mathrm{hv}}_{\mathrm K}\,\xi_n(T_n),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hvac}
\end{equation}
where $\xi_t(T) = [T - T^{\mathrm{hi}}_t]^+ + [T^{\mathrm{lo}}_t - T]^+$ is the
excursion from the comfort band at time $t$, and the heat flow is $q(a) = P\,\mathrm{COP}\,a$
when cooling and $P(\mathrm{COP}+1)\,a$ when heating (Appendix~\ref{app:hvac}).

\paragraph{Electric vehicle} (a battery that cannot discharge, whose charger is
off or at least $P^{\min}$, with a charging goal $G_t$, the energy wanted in store
by slot $t$):
\begin{equation}
  \text{as \eqref{eq:battery} with } \bar P_{\mathrm d} = 0 \text{ and } p_t \in \{0\} \cup [P^{\min}, \bar P_{\mathrm c}],
  \qquad f(p) = \pi_{\mathrm T}\,(s_0 - s_n) + \kappa_{\mathrm{goal}}\textstyle\sum_t [G_t - s_t]^+ .
  \label{eq:ev}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Discomfort in currency.} Restoring one kelvin to a thermal mass $C$
costs $C\bar c/\mathrm{COP}$ at the reference price $\bar c$ (the mean import
price; $\mathrm{COP} = 1$ for a resistive element). A kelvin-hour of discomfort
is priced at $\kappa$ times that,
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{\mathrm K} = \kappa\,C\,\bar c/\mathrm{COP}, \qquad \kappa = 3 \text{ by default,}
  \label{eq:comfort}
\end{equation}
so that the bill and the discomfort are in the same unit and $\kappa$ reads
plainly: how many times the cost of fixing a drift one would pay to avoid it.

The water heater and the HVAC have on/off controls, and the EV's charger a
minimum power, so their $\Pk$ are not convex. The battery, written with separate
charge and discharge variables, is a linear programme (Appendix~\ref{app:lp}).

\subsection{The meter}

The meter carries
\begin{equation}
  z_t = d_t + \chi_t + \sum_{k=1}^m p_{k,t},
  \label{eq:meter}
\end{equation}
positive for import, where $\chi_t \in [0, g_t]$ is PV output curtailed
(discarded). Its cost in slot $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  m_t(z) = \dt\Big(\cimp_t\,[z]^+ - \cexp_t\,[z]^-
         + \cbr\,[z - \zimp]^+ + \cbr\,[-z - \zexp]^+\Big).
  \label{eq:metercost}
\end{equation}
The first two terms are the bill. The last two price energy beyond the import
and export limits at $\cbr = \kappa_{\mathrm g}\max_t
\max(|\cimp_t|,|\cexp_t|)$, with $\kappa_{\mathrm g} = 10$: a price rather than
a hard constraint, so that a plan exists even when a limit cannot be met; a
breach then stays in the plan, priced and reported. (Without a limit, its term
is zero.) Curtailing costs nothing and pays only when export is priced
negatively or beyond the export limit.

When $\cexp_t \le \cimp_t$, $m_t$ is convex and piecewise linear: its slope is
$\cexp_t - \cbr$, $\cexp_t$, $\cimp_t$ and $\cimp_t + \cbr$ (per kWh) from
export beyond the limit to import beyond it. We write
$M_t(y) = \min_{0 \le \chi \le g_t} m_t(y + \chi)$ for the meter cost after the
best curtailment.

\subsection{The optimisation problem}

\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \begin{aligned}
  \min_{p_1,\dots,p_m,\ \chi}\quad & J = \sum_{t=0}^{n-1} m_t(z_t) + \sum_{k=1}^m f_k(p_k) \\
  \text{s.t.}\quad & z_t = d_t + \chi_t + \textstyle\sum_k p_{k,t}, \quad 0 \le \chi_t \le g_t
     && \text{(meter balance, curtailment)} \\
  & p_k \in \Pk,\quad k = 1,\dots,m
     && \text{(device constraints \eqref{eq:battery}--\eqref{eq:ev})}
  \end{aligned}\;}
  \label{eq:P}
\end{equation}
Let $J^\star$ be its minimum. The devices share nothing but the meter. Were
$m_t$ linear --- one price for both directions and no limits --- $J$ would be a
sum of per-device terms and each device could plan alone against the tariff.
The kink of $m_t$ at $z = 0$, where the marginal price jumps from $\cexp_t$ to
$\cimp_t$, and the limits are the entire coupling: whether the next kWh a
device draws is imported, or merely reduces an export, depends on what every
other device does.

Problem \eqref{eq:P} can be written as one mixed-integer linear programme
(MILP), with binaries for the on/off controls and the charger minimum; we use
one as a reference (Appendix~\ref{app:gaps}). As a home controller it has two
drawbacks: it needs every device's model in one place and one formalism, and
its solve time grows with the binaries --- $15$ to $39$~s for one battery and
one water heater over a day. The rest of these notes split \eqref{eq:P} by
device instead.

\subsection{Why devices cannot simply take turns}
\label{sec:bestresponse}

The obvious split is to let each device plan against the others' current plans
and repeat --- all devices at once (a Jacobi iteration) or one at a time
(Gauss--Seidel) \cite{bertsekas1989}. Take two identical batteries and one hour
of PV surplus. Updating together, each sees the surplus and plans to absorb all
of it, so they import to absorb twice the surplus; next round each sees the
expensive import and plans nothing; the round after repeats the first. Updating
one at a time removes this cycle but can stop at a plan that no single device
can improve and yet is not optimal: at the kink, an improvement may need several
devices to move together while the meter stays balanced. A \emph{coordinator} is
needed: something that damps the overshoot and steers the plans to agree.

\section{The device interface}
\label{sec:interface}

Both coordinators talk to a device through the same interface, and neither sees
its model. A device is an object that holds its model, its state and its solver,
and answers queries; the coordinators differ only in which queries they send
(Figure~\ref{fig:arch}).

\paragraph{One query.} Every query asks for the device's cheapest plan under a
charge for its energy:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal Q_k(\pi^+, \pi^-, \delta, v, \rho) \;=\;
  \arg\min_{p \in \Pk}\; f_k(p)
  \;+\; \dt\sum_t\Big(\pi^+_t\,[\delta_t + p_t]^+ - \pi^-_t\,[\delta_t + p_t]^-\Big)
  \;+\; \tfrac{\rho}{2}\,\|p - v\|^2 ,
  \label{eq:query}
\end{equation}
with prices $\pi^+$ for drawing and $\pi^-$ for supplying, a residual load
$\delta$ under which the device's power is metered, and an optional pull of
weight $\rho$ toward a target plan $v$ (with limits, the breach terms of
\eqref{eq:metercost} are added to the energy charge). The answer is the plan $p$,
its private cost $f_k(p)$ and its state trajectory. Three settings of the
parameters are all the coordinators use:
\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.19\textwidth}L{0.3\textwidth}L{0.25\textwidth}L{0.17\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
query & parameters of \eqref{eq:query} & the device minimises & asked by \\
\midrule
price response $\PR_k(\pi)$ & $\pi^+ = \pi^- = \pi$, $\delta = 0$, $\rho = 0$ & $f_k(p) + \dt\,\pi^{\!\top} p$ & Dantzig--Wolfe \\
proximal step $\PX_k(v,\rho)$ & $\pi^\pm = 0$, $\delta = 0$ & $f_k(p) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\|p - v\|^2$ & ADMM \\
best response $\BR_k(\delta)$ & $\pi^+ = \cimp$, $\pi^- = \max(\cexp, 0)$, limits, $\rho = 0$ & $f_k(p) + \sum_t M_t(\delta_t + p_t)$ & both \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In $\BR_k$, $\delta_t = d_t + \sum_{k' \ne k} p_{k',t}$ is the rest of the house,
and pricing export at $\max(\cexp, 0)$ accounts for curtailment
(Proposition~\ref{prop:curtail}). The queries charge the device for its energy in
three ways: at a posted price, not at all but with a pull toward a target, or at
the real tariff given everything else on the meter. A device needs the tariff only
in $\BR$, and never sees the other devices or the grid limits except through
$\delta$ there.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item[DP.] A backward recursion over the device's own
  state, discretised (Appendix~\ref{app:dp}). It suits a device with one or two
  state variables --- its cost grows exponentially with their number, which is
  why it is used per device and never for the whole house. It handles on/off
  controls, charger minima and goals simply by restricting the actions, is exact
  on its grid, and returns besides the plan a value function and a policy for
  every state, which the execution tier uses (Section~\ref{sec:execution}).
\item[LP and QP.] The battery is a linear
  programme: charge, discharge and stored energy with linear constraints. Its
  price response is an LP; its proximal step a convex QP, which the package
  solves exactly by a small interior-point method (Appendix~\ref{app:lp}).
\item[Anything else.] A MILP, a heuristic, a manufacturer's black box: the only
  requirement is that answers be plans the device can run, with their true
  private costs. How well a solver answers decides what the coordinator can
  guarantee.
\end{description}

\subsection{What exact answers buy}
\label{sec:exactness}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Dantzig--Wolfe} accepts any runnable plan as an offer, so an
  inexact solver only slows it. Its certificate (Section~\ref{sec:bound}) needs
  each device's \emph{minimum value} in $\PR$; a solver that may miss the best
  plan must report a lower bound on that value, or the certificate holds only up
  to the solver's error. The DPs are exact on their grids, so here the
  certificate reads ``within the gap, up to the DP grids''.
\item \textbf{ADMM} converges to the optimal cost when every $f_k$ and $\Pk$ is
  convex and every $\PX$ is exact \cite{boyd2011}. On/off devices are not convex
  and DP steps are not exact, so with them ADMM is a heuristic
  (Section~\ref{sec:convergence}); for batteries alone with LP steps, it
  converges to the optimum.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The idea}

Think of the house as a market with one shared good, energy at the meter. A
coordinator posts a price per slot, $\pi_t$; every device answers with its best
plan at those prices, its price response, which we call an \emph{offer}. The
coordinator keeps every offer and chooses weights on them, summing to one per
device, so that the meter balances at the least total cost. Its choice is a
small linear programme whose duals are new prices; the devices answer again,
and the loop repeats. In Dantzig and Wolfe's words, the coordinator ``works out
a system of `prices'\,'', and ``old offers are never forgotten by the central
agency (unless using `current' prices they are unprofitable)''
\cite[p.~102]{dantzigwolfe1960}.

With the two batteries of Section~\ref{sec:bestresponse}, each offers to absorb
the whole surplus. The coordinator takes \emph{half} of each offer: the blend
absorbs the surplus exactly and imports nothing, and the price it posts for that
hour lies between the export and the import price. Offers are only added, so the
coordinator's value can only fall; nothing oscillates. Figure~\ref{fig:flows}
(left) shows the loop.

\subsection{The coordinator's problem}
\label{sec:master}

Let $\mathcal K$ be the devices that bid offers, each with a pool
$\mathcal J_k$ of offers $(p^{kj}, f_{kj})$, and $\mathcal B$ the batteries
held as LP variables. The \emph{master} LP is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min\quad & \dt\sum_t \Big(\cimp_t z^+_t - \cexp_t z^-_t + (\cimp_t + \cbr)\,o^+_t - (\cexp_t - \cbr)\,o^-_t\Big) \\
  & \quad + \sum_{k \in \mathcal K}\sum_{j \in \mathcal J_k} f_{kj}\,w_{kj}
  + \sum_{b \in \mathcal B} \pi_{\mathrm T}\,(s^b_0 - s^b_n) \\
\text{s.t.}\quad
 & z^+_t + o^+_t - z^-_t - o^-_t = d_t + \chi_t + \textstyle\sum_{k,j} w_{kj}\,p^{kj}_t + \sum_b (c^b_t - e^b_t)
   \quad \forall t \qquad [\text{dual } \dt\,\pi_t] \\
 & \textstyle\sum_{j \in \mathcal J_k} w_{kj} = 1 \quad \forall k \in \mathcal K \qquad [\text{dual } \sigma_k] \\
 & s^b_{t+1} = s^b_t + \dt\,(\Ec c^b_t - e^b_t/\Ed) \quad \forall b, t \\
 & 0 \le z^+_t \le \zimp,\ \ 0 \le z^-_t \le \zexp,\ \ o^\pm_t \ge 0,\ \ 0 \le \chi_t \le g_t,\ \ w_{kj} \ge 0, \\
 & 0 \le c^b_t \le \bar P_{\mathrm c},\ \ 0 \le e^b_t \le \bar P_{\mathrm d},\ \ \underline S \le s^b_t \le \bar S .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
The meter row is \eqref{eq:meter} with the flow split into import $z^+$ and
export $z^-$ within the limits and $o^\pm$ beyond them; the objective is
$\sum_t m_t$ plus the offers' and batteries' private costs. A water heater held
in the master adds its dynamics \eqref{eq:tank} at continuous duty in the same
way. Three properties make the master easy to use:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{The kink is exact without binaries.} Import and export are separate
  variables with separate prices; since $\cexp_t \le \cimp_t$, importing and
  exporting in the same slot never pays. (A slot where export pays more than
  import is refused, and the planner falls back to ADMM.)
\item \emph{It is always feasible}: the breach variables absorb any imbalance,
  so any one offer per device starts it.
\item \emph{It is small}: a few hundred rows over a day.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What its prices mean.} The dual of a row is how much the optimal
cost rises if the row asks for one unit more. For the meter row of slot $t$
this is $\pi_t$, the cost to the house of one more kWh drawn then. LP optimality
says a variable in use breaks even at the row prices, so $\pi_t = \cimp_t$ where
the house imports, $\cexp_t$ where it exports, some value in
$[\cexp_t, \cimp_t]$ where the meter is balanced, and $\cimp_t + \cbr$ where it
imports beyond the limit. The same rule on an offer in use gives
$f_{kj} + \dt\,\pi^{\!\top} p^{kj} = \sigma_k$: $\sigma_k$ is what device $k$'s
current blend costs at the current prices.

\subsection{Pricing and the certificate}
\label{sec:bound}

\paragraph{Pricing.} An offer $p$ of device $k$ with private cost $f$ lowers the
master's value if its \emph{reduced cost} is negative,
\begin{equation}
  f + \dt\,\pi^{\!\top} p - \sigma_k < 0 ,
  \label{eq:reduced}
\end{equation}
that is, if at the current prices it is cheaper than the device's current blend.
The offer with the most negative reduced cost is exactly the price response
$\PR_k(\pi)$ of Section~\ref{sec:interface}. If no device has one, the master's
blend is optimal.

\paragraph{The bound.} For \emph{any} prices $\pi \in \R^n$,
\begin{equation}
  L(\pi) \;=\; \dt\,\pi^{\!\top} d
  \;+\; \sum_{k=1}^m \min_{p \in \Pk}\big(f_k(p) + \dt\,\pi^{\!\top} p\big)
  \;+\; \sum_t \min_{\substack{z \in \R \\ 0 \le \chi \le g_t}} \big(m_t(z) - \dt\,\pi_t\,(z - \chi)\big)
  \;\;\le\;\; J^\star .
  \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
The reasoning: at any prices, split the house into independent pieces --- each
device buying its energy at $\pi$, and the meter buying from the grid at the
tariff and reselling at $\pi$ --- and let each piece do its best alone.
Problem~\eqref{eq:P} adds only the rule that the pieces agree on every kWh, which
cannot make anything cheaper. Formally, $L$ is the Lagrangian dual function of
\eqref{eq:P} for its meter rows, and $L(\pi) \le J^\star$ is weak duality, which
holds whether or not the devices are convex \cite[Ch.~5]{boydvandenberghe2004}.
Each device term is the value of its price response; the meter term is closed
form (it is $-\infty$ unless $\pi_t$ lies within the range of $m_t$'s slopes);
a battery held in the master contributes its LP price response.

\paragraph{The certificate.} The coordinator evaluates $L$ at every price it
asks at and keeps the best, $L^{\mathrm{best}}$. For any plan of cost $J$,
\[
  J - J^\star \;\le\; J - L^{\mathrm{best}} \;=:\; \text{gap},
\]
so no plan is cheaper than this one by more than the gap. This holds for any plan,
including one made by ADMM. At the master's own prices, the master's value minus
$L(\pi)$ equals $\sum_k \big(\sigma_k - \min_p (f_k(p) + \dt\,\pi^{\!\top} p)\big)$:
the gap closes exactly when no device has an offer with negative reduced cost.
The bound needs each minimum exactly, or a lower bound on it; the code takes each
device's minimum over its DP's answer and every offer it has made at that price,
and records by how much the DP was undercut.

\subsection{From a blend to a runnable plan}
\label{sec:recover}

The master's answer is a blend: say $60\%$ of one water-heater plan and $40\%$
of another. A modulating device runs its blend through its own physics. For a
battery the meter sees exactly the blended power, and because losses make stored
energy concave in power, the store ends with at least the blended energy. An
on/off element cannot run a blend, nor can an HVAC (a blend could mix heating
with cooling) or an EV whose charger has a minimum power. For those, the last
step picks one offer per device from the pool by re-solving \eqref{eq:master}
with $w_{kj} \in \{0,1\}$: a small MILP over a few dozen offers, solved by the
package's own branch and bound.

\subsection{The algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Dantzig--Wolfe planning cycle (\texttt{dw.coordinator}).}
\label{alg:dw}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require forecasts $\cimp, \cexp, d, g$ and each device's own; bidding devices
  $\mathcal K$, LP batteries $\mathcal B$; tolerance $\epsilon = 10^{-3}$;
  iteration cap ($40$)
\State \textbf{seed:} for each $k \in \mathcal K$, $\mathcal J_k \gets \{$baseline plan,
  $\PR_k(\cimp)$, $\PR_k(\max(\cexp,0))\}$;\quad $L^{\mathrm{best}} \gets -\infty$
\Repeat
  \State solve the master \eqref{eq:master} $\to$ value $J^{\mathrm{LP}}$, weights $w$,
    LP-battery plans, duals $\pi, \sigma$
  \For{each price $\pi'$ in $\{\pi,\ \theta\pi^{\mathrm c} + (1-\theta)\pi\}$}
    \Comment{$\pi^{\mathrm c}$: price of the best bound so far}
    \State for each $k \in \mathcal K$: add $\PR_k(\pi')$ to $\mathcal J_k$ (if new)
    \State evaluate $L(\pi')$ by \eqref{eq:bound}; if $L(\pi') > L^{\mathrm{best}}$:
      $L^{\mathrm{best}} \gets L(\pi')$, $\pi^{\mathrm c} \gets \pi'$
  \EndFor
  \State for each $k \in \mathcal K$: add $\BR_k(\delta_k)$, $\delta_k$ = $d$ + the rest of the
    current blend \Comment{any runnable plan is a valid offer}
\Until{$J^{\mathrm{LP}} - L^{\mathrm{best}} \le \epsilon\max(1, |J^{\mathrm{LP}}|)$, or no new offer, or the cap}
\State \textbf{recover} one runnable plan per device (Section~\ref{sec:recover})
\State \textbf{polish:} up to three sweeps; each $k$ in turn takes $\BR_k$ against the others'
  plans, kept only if $J$ falls
\State \Return plan, its cost $J$, bound $L^{\mathrm{best}}$, gap $J - L^{\mathrm{best}}$, meter prices $\pi$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:dw} is the cycle. The second price in line~4, used from the
second iteration on, is a smoothed one, pulled toward the prices that gave the
best bound so far ($\theta = 0.5$, or $0.8$ when a battery bids offers); any
price gives valid offers and a valid bound, and the smoothed one damps the swings
of the master's duals. The
best-response offers of line~7 help where a device should follow the meter's
balance point, which a single flat price reaches only by blending many offers.
Grid limits and curtailment need no step of their own: they are part of the
master. The testbed also recovers a plan at every iteration and keeps the best.

\paragraph{Cost.} One iteration is one small LP plus, per bidding device, a price
response at each of the two prices and one best response. The master is solved by an interior-point method in numpy
(\texttt{dw.lpsolver}). A day at $15$-minute slots takes $0.6$--$0.9$~s on the
Home Assistant demo site; a week-long evcc request ($672$ slots),
$1.7$--$2.5$~s.

\section{The second coordinator: ADMM}
\label{sec:admm}

ADMM coordinates the same devices without a linear programme. Each device
responds to one shared price and a pull toward its last plan; the price rises
wherever the house plans to draw more than it is supplied. It is the coordinator
for a deployment without an LP solver, and the fallback when Dantzig--Wolfe
cannot run. What it gives up is the certificate, though Dantzig--Wolfe's bound
certifies its plans too.

\subsection{ADMM in brief}

The alternating direction method of multipliers \cite{boyd2011} solves
$\min_{x,y} f(x) + g(y)$ subject to $Ax + By = c$, where $f$ and $g$ are each
easy but their sum is not. With a scaled price $u$ on the constraint, it repeats
three steps:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  x &\leftarrow \arg\min_x\; f(x) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\|Ax + By - c + u\|^2, \\
  y &\leftarrow \arg\min_y\; g(y) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\|Ax + By - c + u\|^2, \\
  u &\leftarrow u + (Ax + By - c) .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:admm}
\end{equation}
The same square appears in both minimisations, and $u$ is the running sum of
past mismatches; $\rho u$ is the price on the constraint. In the form used
below, each step is a \emph{proximal operator},
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{prox}_{f,\rho}(v) = \arg\min_x \; f(x) + \tfrac{\rho}{2}\|x - v\|^2 :
  \label{eq:prox}
\end{equation}
minimise $f$, but stay near $v$; $\rho$ sets how strongly \cite{parikh2014}.

\subsection{The coordinator's problem}
\label{sec:exchange}

Call everything connected to the meter a \emph{terminal} $i = 1,\dots,N$, with
a plan $p_i \in \R^n$ (positive when it draws) and a cost $f_i$:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
terminal & plan & cost $f_i$ & its proximal step \\
\midrule
household load & $p = \ell$ & $0$ & returns $\ell$ \\
PV & $-g \le p \le 0$ (curtailment: $p > -g$) & $0$ & clips $v$ to $[-g, 0]$ \\
grid connection & $p = -z$ & $\sum_t m_t(-p_t)$ & closed form, per slot \\
device $k$ & $p \in \Pk$ & $f_k(p)$ & $\PX_k(v, \rho)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
With these, \eqref{eq:P} is the \emph{exchange problem}
\cite[\S7.3.2]{boyd2011}
\begin{equation}
  \min_{p_1,\dots,p_N} \sum_{i=1}^N f_i(p_i) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
  \sum_{i=1}^N p_{i,t} = 0 \quad \forall t ,
  \label{eq:exchangeproblem}
\end{equation}
whose constraint is the meter balance \eqref{eq:meter}. ADMM on
\eqref{eq:exchangeproblem}, with the plans as $x$ and a copy of them that must
sum to zero as $y$, reduces to two lines \cite[\S7.3.2]{boyd2011}:
\begin{align}
  p_i &\leftarrow \operatorname{prox}_{f_i,\rho}\big(p_i - \bar p - u\big)
    \quad \text{for every terminal } i \text{, in parallel}, \label{eq:pmpstep}\\
  u &\leftarrow u + \bar p, \qquad \bar p_t = \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_i p_{i,t} . \label{eq:pmpprice}
\end{align}
$\bar p$ is the house's imbalance per terminal and $u$ its running sum. Where the
house plans to draw more than it is supplied, $u$ rises and every target
$p_i - \bar p - u$ moves down: the devices draw less, the grid connection
supplies more, at its price. No terminal sees another's plan; they meet only in
$\bar p$ and $u$. Kraning, Chu, Lavaei and Boyd call this \emph{proximal message
passing} \cite{kraning2014}. The grid connection's step is closed form because
$m_t$ is piecewise linear: on each linear piece, the minimiser of cost plus
square is the stationary point clipped to the piece; the step takes the cheapest
of these. The tariff and the limits live in this one step, and no device sees
them.

With the two batteries of Section~\ref{sec:bestresponse}: when both plan to
charge into the same surplus, $\bar p > 0$ in those slots, $u$ rises there, and
both targets move down together, while the square keeps either from jumping.

\paragraph{Stopping and $\rho$.} The residuals are the imbalance
$r_{\mathrm p} = \sqrt N\,\|\bar p\|$ and the movement
$r_{\mathrm d} = \rho\,\|(p - \bar p) - (p - \bar p)^{\mathrm{prev}}\|$ (over all
terminals and slots); the loop stops when both are below $\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}\sqrt{Nn}$,
with $\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}} = 10^{-3}$ kW.
$\rho$ follows the rule of \cite{kraning2014}: with $\omega = \rho\, r_{\mathrm p}/r_{\mathrm d} - 1$,
$\rho \leftarrow \rho\,\exp\!\big(0.01\,\omega + 0.01\,(\omega -
\omega^{\mathrm{prev}})\big)$, $u$ rescaled so that $\rho u$ is unchanged, and
$\rho$ held after $200$ iterations.

\subsection{When it converges, and when it does not}
\label{sec:convergence}

For closed convex costs with a saddle point, and exact proximal steps, the
imbalance vanishes, the total cost converges to its minimum and the prices to
optimal prices \cite[\S3.2]{boyd2011}. The proof needs both. Its key step infers from ``$p$
minimises $f$ plus a square'' that ``$p$ minimises $f$ plus a linear term'',
which holds only for convex $f$ \cite[App.~A]{boyd2011}; and errors in the steps
are tolerated only if summable \cite[\S3.4.4]{boyd2011}. A home breaks both:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{On/off devices are not convex.} Under the square, an on/off element
  can only stay put or jump by its full power, however the price moves.
\item \emph{DP steps are neither exact nor convex.} On its grid a battery's plan
  moves by a whole power step or not at all, so the error never shrinks. One
  battery alone still converges. Several batteries facing one price reach their
  switching points together: they move at once, the same way, overshoot the
  balance, and the price turns them all back. With eight batteries the imbalance
  stalls near $2.5$ against a threshold of $0.04$; a finer grid shrinks the
  stall in proportion but does not remove it (Appendix~\ref{app:admm}).
\end{itemize}
Exact LP steps for the batteries remove the second: each battery's plan then
moves continuously with the price. On a site of batteries alone every cost is
convex and every step exact, and ADMM converges to the optimum, to within what
its stopping test asks --- $0.049$ above the optimum at the default tolerance,
$0.001$ at a hundredth of it (Appendix~\ref{app:admm}). With on/off devices
present it remains a heuristic, so the implementation keeps the best runnable
plan it sees, not the last iterate.

The device steps in \eqref{eq:pmpstep} are simultaneous by design: the plans form
one block, and with $u$ held the problem separates across terminals. Letting each
device see its predecessor's fresh plan would be ADMM with one block per device,
which can diverge even on convex problems \cite{chen2016}.

\subsection{The algorithm}
\label{sec:admmalg}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{ADMM planning cycle, as implemented (\texttt{admm.coordinator}).}
\label{alg:admm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require forecasts; terminals: load, PV, grid connection, and each device, the
  water heater and HVAC as relaxed copies; $\rho_0 = 0.1$; budget $100$ iterations
\State $p_{\mathrm{load}} \gets \ell$, every other $p_i \gets 0$;\quad $u \gets 0$;\quad $\rho \gets \rho_0$
  \Comment{or a warm start}
\For{iteration $= 1,\dots,100$}
  \State targets: $v_i \gets p_i - \bar p - u$ for every terminal $i$
  \State steps, in parallel: load $\ell$; PV $\min(\max(v, -g), 0)$; grid connection in closed form;
    each device $p_k \gets \PX_k(v_k, \rho)$
  \State $\bar p_t \gets \frac1N\sum_i p_{i,t}$;\quad $u \gets u + \bar p$;\quad residuals $r_{\mathrm p}, r_{\mathrm d}$
  \State \textbf{runnable plan:} each relaxed device takes its real device's $\BR$ against the
    others' plans; score by $J$; keep if the best so far
  \State stop if $r_{\mathrm p} \le \epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}\sqrt{Nn}$ and $r_{\mathrm d} \le \epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}\sqrt{Nn}$
  \State update $\rho$ (Section~\ref{sec:exchange}) during the first $200$ iterations
\EndFor
\State \textbf{baseline fallback:} a device takes its baseline plan if that lowers $J$
\State \textbf{polish:} up to three sweeps; each device in turn takes its $\BR$ against the
  others' plans, kept only if $J$ falls
\State \Return the plan (and the pricing re-solve of Section~\ref{sec:execution})
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:admm} departs from proximal message passing as published
\cite{kraning2014} in five ways, each measured in Appendix~\ref{app:admm}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Device steps} are the devices' DPs by default (inexact), a battery's
  optionally its exact LP. No solver is needed by default.
\item \emph{On/off devices iterate as relaxed copies} that may run twelfths of
  a slot, close to the convex relaxations the paper suggests for non-convex
  devices. Without them the plans end three times as far from the bound.
\item \emph{A runnable plan every iteration} (line~6), and the best one kept:
  the copies' plans cannot be run, and the iterates need not settle.
\item \emph{Baseline fallback and polish} at the end; neither can raise $J$. The
  polish carries most of the quality: on twelve test sites the iterations' best
  plan lies $2.0$ above the Dantzig--Wolfe bound, $0.42$ after polishing.
\item \emph{Tuning}: while iterating, the bill's kink is rounded over
  $|z| < 0.25$~kW (scoring uses the exact bill), which makes the price at a
  balanced meter unique; and the budget is $100$ iterations ($300$ come only
  $0.02$ closer, at twice the time).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Warm starts.} A solve can start from where an earlier one stood at its
best plan --- every terminal's plan, $u$ and $\rho$ --- when the devices and
horizon match; for the next planning cycle the state is shifted by the slots that
have passed. Each LP battery step likewise starts from that battery's previous
solution. A warm start reaches a good plan in about a quarter of the iterations,
though not a better final plan (Appendix~\ref{app:admm}).

\paragraph{Cost and quality.} On $72$ test sites ADMM's plans come within $0.51$
of the Dantzig--Wolfe bound on average (Dantzig--Wolfe's own: $0.10$), at about
ten times its time. Against a MILP over the same model it captures
$97.3$--$99.1\%$ of the available savings, Dantzig--Wolfe $98.7$--$99.4\%$
(Figure~\ref{fig:gapsum}).

\section{What a plan comes with: prices}
\label{sec:prices}

\subsection{The meter price}

Dantzig--Wolfe's meter dual $\pi_t$ (Section~\ref{sec:master}) is the cost to the
house of one more kWh drawn in slot $t$, counting every device and every limit.
A flexible load deciding when to run can compare its own value per kWh with it.
It is fixed until the next plan.

\subsection{The value of stored energy}
\label{sec:lambda}

Let $\lambda_t$ be the marginal value of stored energy: how much the best cost of
the rest of the horizon falls per extra kWh in store at the start of slot $t$.
Where it comes from depends on the battery's solver. An LP gives it as the dual
of the storage balance row in \eqref{eq:battery}, along the planned trajectory.
A DP gives it as the slope of its value function, $\lambda_t(s) = \partial
V_t/\partial s$, at \emph{every} state $s$ (Appendix~\ref{app:dp}), which is why
the execution tier uses a DP (Section~\ref{sec:execution}). On the test fixtures
the DP's $\lambda$ lies within $0.007$--$0.032$~cur/kWh of the LP's dual, the
error of its grid.

\paragraph{Reservation prices.} Consider a battery facing a price $c_t$ per kWh
at the meter. The optimality conditions of its LP, with $\lambda_{t+1}$ the dual
of the balance row for slot $t$, say that it charges only if $c_t \le
\Ec\lambda_{t+1}$ (a kWh bought stores $\Ec$ of it), discharges only if $c_t \ge
\lambda_{t+1}/\Ed$ (a kWh sold drains $1/\Ed$), and holds in between, with
equality wherever its power lies strictly inside its limits. So, with
$\lambda$ the value of energy stored during the slot,
\begin{equation}
  \text{import while } c_t < \Ec\lambda, \qquad
  \text{export while } c_t > \lambda/\Ed, \qquad
  \text{hold in between.}
  \label{eq:reservation}
\end{equation}
The band's width $\lambda(1/\Ed - \Ec)$ is the round-trip loss; for a lossless
store it vanishes. The price $c_t$ is the \emph{prevailing} one: $\cimp_t$ while
the house imports, $\cexp_t$ while it exports, since a discharge that displaces
load saves the import price. On a flat tariff with $\cexp = 0.08$, $\cimp =
0.30$ and $\Ec = \Ed = 0.9$, the LP's dual attains $0.08/0.9 = 0.089$ where the
battery charges from surplus and $0.30 \times 0.9 = 0.27$ where it discharges
into the load. Figure~\ref{fig:reservation} shows $\lambda$ and the band on a
dynamic tariff.

\paragraph{The marginal cost of consumption.} A flexible load asks what one more
kWh of demand costs. It is met by the cheapest route,
\begin{equation}
  \pi^{\mathrm{meter}}_t(s) = \min\big\{\cimp_t,\ \ \lambda_t(s)/\Ed,\ \ \cexp_t \text{ if } \delta_t < 0\big\}:
  \label{eq:meterprice}
\end{equation}
buying, drawing on the store, or forgoing export when there is a surplus. With
storage it is often far below $\cimp_t$. It holds the rest of the plan fixed
where a true dual would let it re-optimise; against an LP finite difference its
mean error is $0.0007$--$0.0053$~cur/kWh, worst $0.043$.

\subsection{Prices when devices are on/off}
\label{sec:milpduals}

With on/off controls the problem is a MILP, and no single price vector need
support its optimum. Prices still exist by established constructions: the duals
of the LP left once the integer decisions are fixed \cite{oneill2005}, and
convex-hull (minimum-uplift) prices from the Lagrangian dual \cite{gribik2007}.
Both give a price at the optimal plan. A device acting between plans needs
$\lambda$ at the state it is actually in, which the DP's value function supplies
from a solve performed anyway.

\section{From plan to action}
\label{sec:execution}

A home changes at two speeds, and the package has one tier for each:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Planning}, when the forecasts change (every $5$--$15$ minutes):
  a coordinator plans the horizon (Algorithm~\ref{alg:dw} by default,
  Algorithm~\ref{alg:admm} as the fallback). Then the \emph{pricing re-solve}:
  the first battery's DP at the tariff, against the residual load of the plan,
  $\delta = d + \sum_{k \ne \text{battery}} p_k$, gives a value function
  $V_t(s)$ and a policy $\mathrm{POL}_t(s)$ free of any coordinator's
  artefacts (the proximal term, the master's duals).
\item \textbf{Execution}, on every sensor update: look up the action
  $\mathrm{POL}_t(s)$ and $\lambda_t(s)$ at the \emph{measured} state $s$, by
  interpolation on the stored grid, and publish $\lambda$, the band
  \eqref{eq:reservation}, $\pi^{\mathrm{meter}}$ and the setpoint. No solver
  runs.
\end{itemize}
A battery that has drifted from its forecast thus gets a correct instruction at
once, from the state it is in. This is the one place where the choice of solver
matters beyond the plan: the tier needs a policy over states, which a DP returns;
with an LP the tier would re-solve from each measured state ($8$--$9$~ms for a
battery), making its cadence the solver's.

\section{Who saves what}
\label{sec:ledger}

The house pays one bill, and the plan lowers it; what did the solar and each
device contribute? The bill does not split by device on its own: whether a kWh a
device draws is imported or merely displaces an export depends on everything else
on the meter. The package splits the saving as follows (\texttt{dw.attribution}).

\paragraph{Reimburse, then share.} Compare the plan with a \emph{baseline}: no
PV, the thermal devices on their thermostats, the batteries idle. Device $k$ is
first paid its private-cost change $\Delta f_k = f_k(p_k) - f_k(p^0_k)$, so no
device ends worse off than at its baseline. The rest, the total saving $T$ (bill
saving minus all private-cost changes), is shared among the solar and the
devices.

\paragraph{Among the devices: Aumann--Shapley.} As the devices move together from
their baseline plans $p^0$ to the plan $p$, slot $t$'s flow moves along a line
from $z^0_t$ to $z^1_t$. Each kWh a device shifts is priced at the average price
the house meets along the way \cite{aumannshapley1974},
\[
  \hat c_t = \frac{M_t(z^1_t) - M_t(z^0_t)}{\dt\,(z^1_t - z^0_t)},\qquad
  \text{gain}_k = -\dt\textstyle\sum_t \hat c_t\,\big(p_{k,t} - p^0_{k,t}\big) - \Delta f_k ,
\]
with $\hat c_t$ the slope of $M_t/\dt$ where the flow does not change. No re-optimisation
is needed, and the gains add up to the devices' saving exactly.

\paragraph{Solar and the devices: two orders of arrival.} Pricing slot by slot
misses what storage does with PV --- turn a midday kWh into an evening one --- so
solar is not simply one more player. Instead, average two orders: \emph{PV first},
where solar earns what PV saves on its own, $v_{\mathrm{PV}}$, and the devices
share the rest; and \emph{devices first}, where the devices share what a plan
with no PV saves, $v_{\mathrm D}$, and solar earns the rest. Solar's gain is
$\tfrac12(v_{\mathrm{PV}} + T - v_{\mathrm D})$. This is the Owen value
\cite{owen1977} for two groups, with Aumann--Shapley inside the group of devices;
it needs one extra plan (the devices with no PV) and adds up to $T$ exactly.
Table~\ref{tab:ledger} is an example. Against the Shapley value \cite{shapley1953},
which needs $2^{m+1}$ plans, the rule came within $0.46$ on six test cases, and
within $0.13$ for the batteries (Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}).

\section{Sub-meters and local prices}
\label{sec:submeters}

Some devices do not reach the meter directly. A hybrid inverter puts the PV and
the batteries on a DC bus, and what they send the house passes through the
inverter, with its losses and its rating; a sub-panel puts a heat pump and an EV
charger behind one breaker. Each is a \emph{sub-meter}
(\texttt{types.SubMeter}): a bus $b$ holding the members $\mathcal K_b$ (and, for
a hybrid inverter, the PV), connected to the house's AC bus. Like the grid limits
these couple devices, but only some of them, and the coordinators treat them the
same way: as more rows in the master, priced by their duals.

\subsection{The model}

In slot $t$ the connection sends $x_t \ge 0$ from the bus (bus side) and
delivers $\eta_{\mathrm o} x_t$ to the house, or takes $y_t \ge 0$ (bus side)
and draws $y_t/\eta_{\mathrm i}$ from it. Its AC ratings bound
$\eta_{\mathrm o} x_t \le R_{\mathrm o}$ and $y_t/\eta_{\mathrm i} \le R_{\mathrm i}$;
power beyond them is planned only where nothing else fits, at the breach price
$\cbr$, as a grid limit's. PV on the bus may be clipped, $0 \le \kappa_t \le g_t$,
for free. (Without curtailment allowed, only what the rating forces:
$\kappa_t \le [g_t - R_{\mathrm o}/\eta_{\mathrm o}]^+$; an inverter does that
itself.) The bus balances,
\begin{equation}
  \textstyle\sum_{k \in \mathcal K_b} p_{k,t} + x_t - y_t + \kappa_t = g^b_t
  \qquad [\text{dual } \dt\,\mu^b_t],
  \label{eq:bus}
\end{equation}
with $g^b_t$ the PV on the bus (zero for a sub-panel), and the meter row
\eqref{eq:meter} loses the members and the bus's PV and gains the connection,
$z_t = d'_t + \chi_t + \sum_{k \notin \cup_b \mathcal K_b} p_{k,t}
+ \sum_b (y^b_t/\eta^b_{\mathrm i} - \eta^b_{\mathrm o} x^b_t)$, with $d'_t$ the
load minus the PV left on the AC bus. A shared breaker is the same with
$\eta = 1$ and no PV (\texttt{types.group\_limit}).

\paragraph{The tree.} A node's parent may be another node, so nodes nest: a
breaker behind a panel, a second hybrid inverter on the first one's backup
port. The link variables of node $b$ then enter its parent's balance row
instead of the meter's, with the same coefficients, and \eqref{eq:bus} holds at
every node. Further grid connections (a heat-pump meter) are roots of their
own: each has a balance row of the form \eqref{eq:meter} with its own tariff
and limits, and its own meter variables in the objective. PV arrays are fixed
supplies on the bus they sit on, each clippable there.

\paragraph{Set limits.} A limit on what a set $S$ of devices draws together,
wherever they sit --- a phase, a shared cable --- is one more row per slot,
\[
  \textstyle\sum_{k \in S} p_{k,t} = u^+_t - u^-_t,\qquad 0 \le u^+_t \le \bar u^{\mathrm{imp}},\ 0 \le u^-_t \le \bar u^{\mathrm{exp}}
  \qquad [\text{dual } \dt\,\nu^S_t],
\]
with breach variables beyond the bounds at $\cbr$, as before. Sets may overlap
each other and the tree. A device is now priced at its bus's price plus the
duals of the sets it belongs to, $\mu^{b(k)}_t + \sum_{S \ni k} \nu^S_t$:
$\nu^S$ is zero while the limit has headroom and a premium while it binds.

Everything that scores a plan
--- both coordinators, the fast tier, the saving split --- computes the meter
from the devices' powers through one function (\texttt{submeter.site\_meter}),
which makes the connection's best use slot by slot exactly as these rows do.

\subsection{Local prices}

The dual $\mu^b_t$ of \eqref{eq:bus} is what one more kWh drawn on the bus costs
the house: the bus's \emph{local price}. A device on the bus is priced at it (plus
its set limits' premiums) instead of at $\pi_t$ --- its reduced cost \eqref{eq:reduced} reads
$f + \dt\,\mu^{b\top} p - \sigma_k$ --- and the bound \eqref{eq:bound} gains,
per bus, $-\dt\,\mu^{b\top} g^b$ and the connection's own terms
$\min_{x,y,\kappa}$ over its boxes, closed form like the meter's. Nothing else
changes: $L(\pi, \mu)$ is still the Lagrangian dual function, now for the meter
rows and the bus rows together, and $L \le J^\star$ is still weak duality.

LP optimality pins $\mu^b_t$ down from $\pi_t$ (writing $\eta_{\mathrm o}$,
$\eta_{\mathrm i}$ for bus $b$'s):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item sending, below the rating: $\mu^b_t = \eta_{\mathrm o}\,\pi_t$ --- a kWh on
  the bus is worth what it fetches at the meter, less the inverter's loss;
\item taking, below the rating: $\mu^b_t = \pi_t/\eta_{\mathrm i}$;
\item neither: $\eta_{\mathrm o}\pi_t \le \mu^b_t \le \pi_t/\eta_{\mathrm i}$, the
  connection's own no-trade band;
\item sending at the rating: $\mu^b_t \le \eta_{\mathrm o}\pi_t$, and while PV is
  clipped, $\mu^b_t = 0$ --- one more kWh on the bus would be clipped too.
\end{itemize}
The last case is what the rows are for: at midday, with the inverter full, a
battery behind it is offered energy at $0$ while the meter's price is still the
export price, so it stores the PV that would otherwise be clipped even when
exporting would pay. These are nodal prices in the sense of spot pricing
\cite{schweppe1988}, with the inverter as a lossy, rated line.

\paragraph{Which dual.} While a connection carries nothing, its bus's price is not
unique, and the band can be far wider than the one above: behind a panel that
may not feed back (a sending rating of $0$), with nothing on it drawing, any
$\mu^b_t$ from $\eta_{\mathrm o}\pi_t - \cbr$ up to $\pi_t/\eta_{\mathrm i}$ is
optimal for the restricted master. A solver returns one end; at the bottom, the
loads there are priced far below the meter, bid plans for slots they will
never get, and the loop chases that price slot by slot --- forty iterations
without closing the gap on a house like the EMHASS example's, six with the
fix. So after each master solve the duals are also moved, within the set
of optimal ones, to the point nearest the pass-through prices: each node's
$\mu^b_t$ clipped to $[\eta_{\mathrm o}\pi^{a}_t, \pi^{a}_t/\eta_{\mathrm i}]$ for
its parent's price $\pi^a_t$, each set limit's $\nu^S_t$ toward $0$. A node moves
together with every node below it whose connection is tight, scaled by that
connection's efficiency, and only as far as every reduced cost keeps its sign
(a variable strictly inside its bounds pins the price): the vector stays an
optimal dual. The loop prices at it as well as at the solver's own (any
point gives valid columns and a valid bound, and the best bound is kept; an
interior point's duals, near the centre of the optimal set, are often the
better guide elsewhere), and reports it as the local prices: it is the price a
load behind the panel actually faces, what one more kWh drawn there costs.

\paragraph{One relaxation.} Sending and taking at once loses energy, and never
pays while energy has a non-negative value; with curtailment allowed, clipping
dominates it anyway. The LP does not forbid it --- EMHASS uses a binary
per slot --- just as it does not forbid a battery charging and discharging at
once. Where energy at a node has no outlet --- prices negative and curtailment off,
or a battery on a converter under a panel that may not feed back - the master
can use it to burn energy, and its plan would not be one a converter can run.
Recovery therefore checks the plan it returns: wherever a link both sends and
takes in a slot, that slot's direction is fixed to the net flow and the plan
recovered again. The bound is untouched (the master stays a relaxation); on the
test sites the fixed plan cost the same as the relaxed one, the loop having
been one of several optima.

\subsection{In ADMM and in the fast tier}

In proximal message passing the house is a network \cite{kraning2014}: each bus is
a net with its own price and average, its members' terminals and the PV's on it,
and the connection is a device with one terminal on each net, $(s, -\eta_{\mathrm o}s)$
for $s \ge 0$ and $(s, -s/\eta_{\mathrm i})$ for $s < 0$. Its proximal step
minimises over four pieces of $s$, each a quadratic with a closed-form minimiser,
clipped to the piece. A set limit is a net too: its limit is a terminal free
within the bounds and at $\cbr$ beyond them, and each member device has a
second terminal on it, tied to its first, so the device's step pulls toward the
mean of its terminals' targets with $\rho$ per terminal. A device DP behind a bus (\texttt{meter.Bus}) scores each
action through the connection, so the fast tier's $\lambda$ and its actions
respect the rating.

\subsection{Who saves what, at local prices}

Section~\ref{sec:ledger} priced each kWh a device shifts at the average slope of
the meter cost along the path from the baseline to the plan. With sub-meters the
bill in slot $t$ is a function $C_t(u)$ of several totals --- what each bus draws
(the main meter's, each node's, each further connection's) and each set limit's
members' total --- and Aumann--Shapley prices a device's kWh at
the average, along the same straight path $u(s) = u^0 + s\,(u^1 - u^0)$, of the
bill's slope in \emph{its} bus's total:
\[
  \hat c^b_t = \frac1\dt\int_0^1 \frac{\partial C_t}{\partial u_b}\big(u(s)\big)\,ds,
  \qquad
  \text{gain}_k = -\dt\textstyle\sum_t \big(\hat c^{b(k)}_t + \sum_{S \ni k} \hat c^S_t\big)\big(p_{k,t} - p^0_{k,t}\big) - \Delta f_k ,
\]
a device counting in its bus's total and in each of its set limits'.
Since $\sum_b \dt\,\hat c^b_t\,(u^1_b - u^0_b) = C_t(u^1) - C_t(u^0)$ by the
fundamental theorem of calculus along the path, the gains still add up to the
devices' saving exactly. With one bus this is the secant of
Section~\ref{sec:ledger}. $C_t$ is piecewise linear, so its gradient is constant
between kinks; the code (\texttt{dw.attribution.\_bus\_prices}) cuts each slot's
path into cells, adds a cell's gradient times its length where the gradient is
the same at both ends, and halves the others, so the integral is exact up to
rounding (each cell's contribution is corrected to its exact change in the
bill). The Owen value between solar and the devices is unchanged.

The effect is the one the local prices suggest. Table~\ref{tab:hybrid} sweeps
the inverter's rating on the demo site (9~kW of PV, a 10~kWh battery and an

rises --- it is credited, at its bus's price, for clipped PV it stores --- then
falls once the rating also limits what it can deliver to the house in the
evening.

\section{What is guaranteed, and what is not}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Guaranteed.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item Dantzig--Wolfe's gap bounds how far any plan is from $J^\star$, up to the
  exactness of the price responses (the DP grids).
\item On a site whose devices are all convex, with exact steps, ADMM converges to
  the optimum (batteries alone, with LP steps).
\item Every state bound holds by construction, with no clipping: the battery's
  admissible power keeps $s$ in $[\underline S, \bar S]$, and the water heater's
  outflow cap and cut-out keep $T$ within its range (Appendix~\ref{app:models}).
\item Curtailing after the device solves is exact when devices are priced at
  $\max(\cexp, 0)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:curtail}).
\item The reservation prices \eqref{eq:reservation} follow from the battery's
  optimality conditions.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Not guaranteed.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{ADMM with on/off devices or DP steps} is a heuristic with no
  certificate of its own; Dantzig--Wolfe's bound certifies its plans.
\item \textbf{$\lambda$ is conditional}: solved against the other devices'
  planned powers, it is a marginal value given those plans, not a system-wide
  dual. Two batteries on one meter need not report the same $\lambda$.
\item \textbf{$\lambda$ carries the grid's error} ($0.007$--$0.032$~cur/kWh); a
  $\pm 0.04$ band is published with it, and decisions inside it should not be
  automated.
\item \textbf{A plan may carry a small priced breach}: Dantzig--Wolfe meets a
  limit or reports the breach; ADMM may leave a few watts over it where a
  device's grid has no step that lands on the headroom (at most $5$~W on the
  test sites).
\item \textbf{The execution tier follows the storage state, not the meter.} A
  deviation that moves the battery's state is tracked; one that does not --- a
  large load switching on while the battery idles --- changes nothing until the
  next plan. Limits are respected in the plan, not enforced live. Clipping
  setpoints against the measured meter is an option (\texttt{policy.clamp}); with
  several batteries it must allocate the headroom sequentially
  (\texttt{clamp\_fleet}), or it double-counts it as in
  Section~\ref{sec:bestresponse}. Only the first battery gets a live policy;
  others follow their planned powers until the next plan.
\item \textbf{Model simplifications.} The water heater is one well-mixed node (a
  real tank stratifies, so the model understates deliverable comfort during a
  reheat); its surroundings and mains temperatures are constants; transitions
  are explicit Euler steps; and treating the element as on/off is a modelling
  choice, since over $15$ minutes a thermostat cycles many times.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The residential scheduling problem in its modern form --- a horizon, a price
forecast, appliance models, energy cost traded against discomfort --- dates to
Mohsenian-Rad and Leon-Garcia \cite{mohsenianrad2010}; its multi-household version
coordinates homes through a shared price \cite{mohsenianrad2010dsm}. The
literature mostly solves it as one MILP \cite{hemsreview2022}. The first
coordinator here is the decomposition principle of Dantzig and Wolfe
\cite{dantzigwolfe1960}, in the form the paper calls a generalised programme:
offers may come from any set, so the devices need not be linear programmes. The
second is ADMM \cite{boyd2011} in exchange form, which for networks of energy
devices is proximal message passing \cite{kraning2014}; ADMM has also coordinated
electric-vehicle fleets \cite{rivera2013}. With discrete devices ADMM is a
heuristic: its convergence results for nonconvex problems rest on smoothness an
on/off element lacks \cite{wang2019}, and ``run, keep the best point, polish'' is
the recipe used for nonconvex sets generally \cite{diamond2018}. The marginal
value of stored energy comes from hydro scheduling, where the slope of a
future-cost function prices stored water \cite{pereira1991,philpott2017}; for
end-user storage the optimal policy is of threshold type, with thresholds moving
with price \cite{vandeven2012}, which is the band \eqref{eq:reservation}. Prices
under nonconvexity follow the electricity-market literature
\cite{oneill2005,gribik2007}, and the savings split cooperative game theory
\cite{shapley1953,aumannshapley1974,owen1977}. What is specific here is the
interface: both coordinators run the same devices through the same three
queries, with the solver behind each chosen per device.

\section{Device models}
\label{app:models}

\subsection{Battery}
\label{app:battery}

The balance \eqref{eq:battery} holds exactly, with no saturation, because the
admissible power already respects the store's bounds:
\begin{equation}
  p_t \in \Big[-\bar P_{\mathrm d},\ \bar P_{\mathrm c}\Big] \cap
  \Big[-\tfrac{(s_t - \underline S)\,\Ed}{\dt},\ \tfrac{\bar S - s_t}{\Ec\,\dt}\Big].
  \label{eq:actionset}
\end{equation}
Filling the headroom $\bar S - s$ needs meter power $(\bar S - s)/(\Ec\dt)$,
more than $(\bar S - s)/\dt$; drawing down to the reserve allows only
$(s - \underline S)\Ed/\dt$. The admissible power shrinks as either bound
approaches, so $s_t$ never leaves $[\underline S, \bar S]$. A reserve above the
starting state is lowered to it: the battery may charge but not discharge
further. The terminal price $\pi_{\mathrm T}$ is linear so that $\partial
f/\partial s_n$ stays a price at the horizon's edge; the library uses the lowest
import price, and the testbed the mean, which stops a battery emptying itself by
the end of the day at the cost of the opposite lean.

\subsection{Water heater}
\label{app:tank}

A single well-mixed node: uniform temperature $T$, constant density $\rho_w$ and
specific heat $c_p$, and every litre drawn replaced by mains water at
$T^{\mathrm{in}}$. With $C = \rho_w c_p V$, element power $P$, insulation
resistance $R$ to the air around the tank at $T^{\mathrm{surr}}$, and a draw of
$W_t$ m$^3$/h, conservation of energy gives
\begin{equation}
  C\,\dot T = P\alpha - \frac{T - T^{\mathrm{surr}}}{R} - \rho_w c_p W\,(T - T^{\mathrm{in}}).
  \label{eq:tankode}
\end{equation}
The two losses reference different temperatures, as they must: the insulation
leaks to the room, the tap is refilled from the main, and a tank run flat
settles at mains temperature. A draw forecast is given as the heat it removes at
the setpoint, $D_t = \rho_w c_p W_t (T^\star - T^{\mathrm{in}})$ [kW]. With
$\beta = 1/(RC)$, $\gamma_t = D_t/(C(T^\star - T^{\mathrm{in}}))$ and the
equilibrium $T^\infty_t = (\beta T^{\mathrm{surr}} + \gamma_t T^{\mathrm{in}})/(\beta
+ \gamma_t)$, the tank relaxes toward $T^\infty_t$ at rate $\beta + \gamma_t$.
Over a slot the heat leaving is
\begin{equation}
  Q^{\mathrm{out}}_t(T) = C\,\min\{\beta + \gamma_t,\ 1/\dt\}\,(T - T^\infty_t),
  \label{eq:outflow}
\end{equation}
and the state moves by \eqref{eq:tank}. The cap lets the tank relax at most to
$T^\infty_t$ in one slot, so with the element off $T_{t+1}$ lies between
$T^\infty_t$ and $T_t$, and $\min_t T^\infty_t$ is an exact floor for the state
grid. (At the default parameters, $C = 0.174$~kWh/K, $R = 80$~K/kW, a peak draw of
$4$~kW, $(\beta + \gamma_t)\dt = 0.16$, so the cap is inactive.) The thermostat
cut-out $\overline T$ bounds the \emph{action}:
\begin{equation}
  \bar\alpha_t(T) = \min\Big\{1,\ \frac{(\overline T - T)\,C/\dt + Q^{\mathrm{out}}_t(T)}{P}\Big\},
  \label{eq:duty}
\end{equation}
and a commanded on realises $\alpha_t = \bar\alpha_t(T_t)$ in the slot that
reaches the cut-out. No action carries the tank past $\overline T$; over draws
from $1\times$ to $200\times$ the fixture it stays within $[15, 75]$~$^\circ$C
with nothing clipped.

\subsection{Heat pump / HVAC}
\label{app:hvac}

The same single-node balance with the heat pump in place of the element and no
draw: the room exchanges heat with the outdoor air through the envelope and by
infiltration, which combine into one resistance $R_{\mathrm{wall}}$, giving
\eqref{eq:hvac}. Heating delivers $\mathrm{COP} + 1$ times the electrical power
(the compressor work also ends up in the room), cooling removes $\mathrm{COP}$
times it. The duty is capped as in \eqref{eq:duty}, at the fraction that reaches
the top of the modelled range when heating or its bottom when cooling. The end
term prices the final excursion from the comfort band for one hour.

\subsection{Electric vehicle}

evcc describes a charging point as a battery that only charges, with a
per-slot charge floor, a charger minimum (off or at least $P^{\min}$) and an
energy goal. The goal is soft, priced at $\kappa_{\mathrm{goal}}$ per kWh of
shortfall per slot ($100$ by default), so an unreachable goal degrades to ``as
close as possible''. A DP enforces the charger minimum by deleting actions; an LP
would need one binary per slot, which is why an EV bids offers rather than
sitting in the master.

\section{Device solvers}
\label{app:solvers}

\subsection{Dynamic programming}
\label{app:dp}

A device with state $x$ on a grid $\mathcal X$ and actions $a$ on a grid
$\mathcal A(x)$ (restricted to what the state allows, as in
\eqref{eq:actionset} and \eqref{eq:duty}), transition $x_{t+1} = F_t(x_t, a_t)$
and power $p(a)$ answers a query by the backward recursion
\begin{equation}
  V_n(x) = -\Phi(x), \qquad
  V_t(x) = \max_{a \in \mathcal A(x)} \Big\{ -\,\text{cost}_t(x, a) + V_{t+1}\big(F_t(x,a)\big) \Big\},
  \label{eq:bellman}
\end{equation}
with $V_{t+1}$ interpolated between grid points, followed by a forward rollout
from the measured state \cite{bertsekas2017}. $\Phi$ is the end term of $f$, and
the stage cost is the device's own (discomfort, goal shortfall) plus the query's
charge for its energy:
\[
  \PR:\ \dt\,\pi_t\,p(a); \qquad
  \PX:\ \tfrac{\rho}{2}\big(p(a) - v_t\big)^2; \qquad
  \BR:\ m_t\big(\delta_t + p(a)\big) \text{ with } \max(\cexp_t, 0) \text{ for } \cexp_t .
\]
The cost is $O(n\,|\mathcal X|\,|\mathcal A|)$ per solve. Defaults: battery
$200$ energy levels and $81$ power levels (the testbed, $50$ levels); water heater
$68$ temperatures; HVAC $40$. The relaxed copies ADMM iterates allow $13$ duty
levels. Besides the plan, a solve returns $V_t(x)$ and the policy
$\mathrm{POL}_t(x)$ at every grid state; $\partial V_t/\partial s$ is $\lambda$.

\subsection{The battery as an LP, and its proximal step}
\label{app:lp}

With charge $c_t \in [0, \bar P_{\mathrm c}]$, discharge $e_t \in [0, \bar
P_{\mathrm d}]$, stored energy $s_t \in [\underline S, \bar S]$ and $p = c - e$,
the battery is the LP
\begin{equation}
  s_{t+1} = s_t + \dt\,(\Ec c_t - e_t/\Ed), \qquad
  \PR:\ \min\ \dt\,\pi^{\!\top}(c - e) - \pi_{\mathrm T}\,s_n ,
  \label{eq:batterylp}
\end{equation}
which the master \eqref{eq:master} holds as variables. Charging and discharging
in one slot is allowed; it only wastes energy, so an optimum does it only where
wasting energy costs nothing. The proximal step replaces the objective by
$\tfrac{\rho}{2}\|c - e - v\|^2 - \pi_{\mathrm T}\,s_n$, a convex QP. The package
solves it by a primal--dual interior-point method with Mehrotra's
predictor--corrector (\texttt{admm.battery\_qp}); the Newton system
reduces to a tridiagonal one in the $n$ balance duals, so an iteration costs
$O(n)$. A warm start moves the previous solution $5\%$ of each range off its
bounds and floors its bound multipliers, which saves about a third of the
iterations with the same answer.

bound, against $0.51$ on the real sites. The grid steps account for roughly half
of ADMM's distance from the bound; the on/off devices for the other half.

\section{Curtailment and the price devices see}
\label{app:curtail}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:curtail}
Let curtailment be free up to the PV output $g_t$, and let no plan export more
than $g_t$ in a slot where $\cexp_t < 0$. Then planning each device's best
response priced at $\max(\cexp_t, 0)$ and curtailing afterwards is exact: it is
the best response to $M_t$. Priced at the raw $\cexp_t$ it is not.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} Curtailment raises $z_t$ only where the house exports, and
reduces export one for one, so it changes neither the residual load nor any
device's feasible set. It changes the value of the alternatives. With curtailment
available, a kWh of PV surplus is never exported at a negative price, because
discarding it costs nothing; so the revenue a device forgoes by absorbing a unit
of surplus is $\max(\cexp_t, 0)$, which is the slope of $M_t$ there. Priced at a
negative $\cexp_t$, a device is credited $|\cexp_t|$ per kWh for absorbing surplus
that could be discarded for free, and pays real conversion losses for a benefit
that does not exist. Where $\cexp_t \ge 0$ the two prices agree.
\hfill$\square$

\smallskip\noindent
Measured on the day/night fixture with a $-0.10$ midday export price, a battery
priced at the raw tariff costs $0.31$ more per day, and cycles $7.3$~kWh more,
than priced at $\max(\cexp, 0)$; the difference grows linearly as the price falls
and vanishes for $\cexp \ge 0$. An export \emph{cap} needs no correction: its
breach price already prices it, and absorbing surplus into storage beats
discarding it whenever there is headroom.
